# Spec: grok-audit-surface

## Executive Summary
Measure grok's audit recall and fabrication rate against agy on a fixed seeded-defect set, publish the result either way, and only if grok clears the D3 bar against agy (fabrication rate no higher, recall no lower, and recall strictly higher when the fabrication rates are equal, so a full tie does not promote), make grok a standing third leg on a document's first gating cycle (`--surfaces codex,agy,grok`), leaving revision cycles, the exit streak and the round cap as they behave today.

## Goal
Measure grok as an audit surface, ending the **unmeasured** label on its audit precision, and add a third model family to each document's first gate when, and only when, the measurement supports it.

## Decisions carried in (operator-approved 2026-10-18, binding)
- **D1 Role.** grok becomes a standing third leg on a document's FIRST gating cycle only. Revision cycles are unchanged: codex gates and the `doc-auditor` does an ADVISORY delta review. Out of scope: grok as implementer, grok as doc author, and a grok TDD write gate.
- **D2 Calibration.** A seeded-defect set built from copies of archived phase documents that have already been audited, with a ground-truth label per seed. grok and agy run on identical seeded prompts through the normal audit-cycle path, and codex runs as the reference ceiling when it is available. The scorer is a committed probe, the result is a committed measurement doc, and both can be re-run after a grok upgrade.
- **D3 Promotion bar (amended by operator decision, 2026-10-18: strict tie rule).** Compared with agy on the same seeds, grok passes only when its fabrication rate is ≤ agy's AND its recall is ≥ agy's, AND, when the two fabrication rates are equal, grok's recall is STRICTLY greater than agy's. Stated as one predicate: `(fab_grok < fab_agy AND recall_grok ≥ recall_agy) OR (fab_grok = fab_agy AND recall_grok > recall_agy)`. A full tie (equal fabrication rate and equal recall) does NOT promote. The amendment supersedes the looser reading of the original D3 (≤ and ≥ alone, under which a full tie passed) and adopts the brainstorm's Risks-row rule. Every statement of the bar in this spec (Executive Summary, AC-5.3, AC-5.5, FR-6, FR-7) refers to this predicate. On a miss grok stays fallback-only, and the numbers are still published. "Unmeasured" is retired either way. The routing change (FR-7) ships only on a pass.
- **D4 Re-smoke (amended by operator decision, 2026-10-19: version-agnostic).** The re-smoke covers the AUDIT PATH ONLY. The host-mode adapter is out of scope. The audit-path re-smoke runs against the grok on PATH at S1, whatever its version; the smoke records that binary's path and its `grok --version` line, and the measurement doc names that version. No phase document names a target grok version. (Decision record: `docs/03-analysis/grok-audit-surface.decision-2026-10-19-d4.md`.)

## Functional Requirements

### FR-1: Audit-path re-smoke on the recorded grok version (D4)
- **Description**: Before any calibration dispatch, run one real grok audit pass through `hmad-dispatch audit-cycle --passes 1 --surfaces grok` against one seeded document from FR-2, in that document's AC-2.6 export root, and record what the audit path actually does on the grok on PATH, together with that binary's path and its `grok --version` line (D4). The smoke covers four things: the `exec grok` audit dispatch, the NDJSON event types, `scan_grok` evidence, and report-file delivery. It covers nothing in host mode (hooks, `spawn_subagent`, the TDD gate, session id).
- **Acceptance Criteria**:
  - AC-1.1: The smoke's pass log holds at least one `end` event, and the cycle's `Effort:` block reports `format=grok` (shape `grok`), not `grok-truncated`. A `grok-truncated` reading fails the smoke. It is not retried as a pass.
  - AC-1.2: Every distinct `type` value in the live log is listed in the measurement doc and classified as in-vocabulary or out-of-vocabulary against BOTH `_GROK_TYPES_RE` (`hmad-dispatch.sh`) and `_GROK_TYPES` (`h_mad_review_evidence.py`). If the two vocabularies differ from each other at the smoke sha, the smoke fails. An out-of-vocabulary type does not fail the smoke, but it is published together with whether it carries tool or usage data. If `end`, `tool_call` or `tool_call_update` is missing, the smoke fails.
  - AC-1.3: A pass **delivers a report** when its value in the `delivered=` field of the cycle's `AUDITCYCLE:` line is `report-file` or `out`, AND `h_mad_audit_gate.py`, run on that pass's collected report, prints `GATE: PASS` or `GATE: FAIL`. Both forms count: `report-file` is the agent's report file, and `out` is a complete report that the collector extracted from the agent's captured output, wrote as the collected report, and the gate then scored. A pass whose value is `none` or `unscorable:<reason>`, whose collected report is absent, or whose gate run prints any other token, has not delivered. The `delivered=` field is the only delivery input on the audit-cycle path, which prints no `COLLECT:` line (Premises). A verdict word read from grok's final message, without a collected report that the gate scored, does not count.
  - AC-1.4: The measurement doc records the `RESOLVED-MODEL` line from `h_mad_resolved_model.py grok --log <pass log>`, the pass's wall time, and the `--timeout` chosen for FR-3. The timeout is derived as `ceil(1.5 × measured wall seconds / 60) × 60` with a floor of the wrapper's default 900 s, and the derivation is printed beside the result. The re-smoke itself runs with `--timeout 1800`, so the measured wall time is not clipped at that floor (operator ruling 2026-10-23, after run 5 `run-ab2c85dc2e25-20261023T070009Z`. Under the 900 s default, its grok re-smoke was killed with rc=124 at 909 s and the smoke failed `truncated`; recorded grok re-smoke walls are 678 s, 863 s, and over 900 s).
  - AC-1.5: `h-mad/references/grok-runtime.md` §"Version and compatibility" gains one sentence saying that the audit path (the `exec grok` audit leg, the event vocabulary, `scan_grok` and report delivery) was re-smoked against grok `<version>`, where `<version>` is the version the smoke recorded from `grok --version` (D4), and naming the measurement doc. The existing sentence "These mappings were checked against grok 1.0.41." stays **unchanged**, because the host-mode mappings it covers were not re-checked (D4). This sentence ships only when the smoke passes (AC-1.7) and the score is not `UNREADABLE` (see "Delivery by outcome").
  - AC-1.6: Non-git root acceptance is MEASURED, not assumed. In the same export root as the grok smoke, S1 also runs one `hmad-dispatch audit-cycle --passes 1 --surfaces agy` cycle, and one `--passes 1 --surfaces codex` cycle when codex is available. codex's `--skip-git-repo-check` is a claim about the code and is measured the same way, not taken as given. A surface **accepts** the root when its pass delivers a report (the AC-1.3 evidence) AND the cycle's `AUDITCYCLE:` is not `UNVERIFIED`, so the agent read files inside the root and did not merely answer. A surface **refuses** the root on anything else: a non-zero agent exit with no delivered report, a delivered report on an `UNVERIFIED` cycle, or agent output that rejects the working directory. A refusal by grok or agy is a smoke failure with reason `nongit_root_refused:<agent>`. A codex refusal is published, and FR-3 then runs the codex-unavailable shape (`--passes 2 --surfaces agy,grok`) with the codex ceiling published as not run. **No surface ever falls back to a git work tree or a worktree root.** A refusal halts or drops that surface. It never re-runs the cycle in a root where git history is reachable.
  - AC-1.7: The smoke ends on one line in the measurement doc: `SMOKE: PASS` or `SMOKE: FAIL reason=<r>[,<r>…]`. It is derived from each S1 cycle's recorded `AUDITCYCLE:` line (its verdict word, its `delivered=` field and its `Effort:` block), the `GATE:` token of each delivered pass's collected report (AC-1.3), and the pass logs, never typed by hand, and never from a `COLLECT:` line, which the audit-cycle path does not print (Premises). The design names the component that derives it. `reason=` lists every failing check, in this order:
    - `truncated`: the grok log is `grok-truncated` (AC-1.1).
    - `vocab_split`: `_GROK_TYPES_RE` and `_GROK_TYPES` differ from each other at the smoke sha (AC-1.2).
    - `vocab_missing:<type>`: `end`, `tool_call` or `tool_call_update` is absent from the live log, one reason per missing type (AC-1.2).
    - `no_delivery`: the AC-1.3 evidence is absent for the grok pass.
    - `nongit_root_refused:<agent>`, where `<agent>` is `grok` or `agy` (AC-1.6).

    There is no other reason value. An out-of-vocabulary type is published, and it is not a reason (AC-1.2).

    **Coupled reasons.** Each reason is read from its own observation: `truncated` from the grok pass's `Effort:` item, `vocab_split` from the two vocabulary sources, `vocab_missing:<type>` from the log's distinct `type` set, `no_delivery` from the AC-1.3 evidence, and `nongit_root_refused:<agent>` from that surface's AC-1.6 acceptance. The axis is *one reason whose trigger implies another reason's trigger*, and it has exactly two kinds:
    - **Implied by this spec's own definitions**, which no fixture can separate. There is one member: `no_delivery` implies `nongit_root_refused:grok`, because grok accepts the root only when its pass delivers a report (AC-1.6 uses the AC-1.3 evidence). Every grok `no_delivery` is therefore printed as `no_delivery,nongit_root_refused:grok` (with any other failing reasons in their places in the order above).
    - **Coupled only through an authority on real runs**, which a synthetic fixture can separate because each reason reads its own observation: a `grok-truncated` log has no `end` event (so `truncated` comes with `vocab_missing:end`) and gives `UNVERIFIED reason=low_evidence_unmeasurable:pN` (so it comes with `nongit_root_refused:grok`). The members are `truncated`, `vocab_missing:end` and `nongit_root_refused:grok`.

    Residual: a coupling introduced later by a change to `scan_grok`, `combine` or `collect` is of the second kind and is not listed here until the spec is revised.

    **Exit contract**, in the same style as AC-5.1: the component prints its token on stdout, and callers read the token, never `$?`. `SMOKE: PASS` and `SMOKE: FAIL reason=…` are both verdicts, and both exit 0. A non-zero exit is reserved for an operational error, where the inputs could not be read. In that case the component exits 2 and prints `SMOKE: UNREADABLE reason=<r>`. That token carries neither PASS nor FAIL, and `<r>` is exactly one of two values:
    - `input_missing:<path>`: a pass log, a cycle's recorded `AUDITCYCLE:` line, or a delivered pass's collected report, that the derivation needs, is absent.
    - `input_unparseable:<path>`: such an input is present, but it cannot be parsed. This includes the grok cycle's recorded `auditcycle.txt` when its `Effort:` block does not parse (two `Effort:` lines, an item not beginning `p<N> `, or an `Effort:` line with no items), and, on a cycle that dispatched, when the grok pass's `p1` item is a form the smoke cannot measure: either of the wrapper's two wrong-file forms (`p1 unreadable (log named but not readable)`, and an item beginning `p1 not measured (codex-text log`), or any item form the design does not name. On an unexpected failure, `<path>` is the input being read when it failed; when no input was open, it is the `--data` value given to the smoke, and it is empty when none was given (a usage error without `--data`, AC-5.1).

    There is no other UNREADABLE reason. An UNREADABLE smoke is not a smoke failure, and it is never written to the measurement doc as `SMOKE: FAIL`. S2 does not start, and nothing in "Delivery by outcome" ships on its account. The operator restores the input and derives again from the same passes. If the passes themselves are lost, the operator runs a new smoke, which is a new reading.

    **What the restore remedy cannot clear.** Restoring an input clears only a copy that was damaged or lost after the pass wrote it. The axis is *whether the refused bytes are the bytes the S1 run itself produced*. When they are, deriving again from the same passes gives the same token, and only a new smoke, which is a new reading, clears it. The members are exactly two: a grok `Effort:` item that the wrapper itself printed into `auditcycle.txt` in a refused form (the two wrong-file forms and any unnamed form above), and a grok pass log that was itself missing, empty or codex text when the pass ended. Residual: the probe cannot tell a restored intact copy from a damaged one, so the operator settles which member applies by deriving once from the restored copy. If the token is unchanged, the operator runs a new smoke.

    **Fixtures.** Each of the two UNREADABLE reasons, `PASS`, and each `FAIL` reason gets its own fixture test, which plants that one reason's trigger and no other, and asserts the exact `reason=` list the component prints. For every reason except `no_delivery`, that list is the reason alone. The `no_delivery` fixture plants only an absent grok delivery (its cycle not `UNVERIFIED`) and asserts exactly `reason=no_delivery,nongit_root_refused:grok`, the coupling of the first kind above. `nongit_root_refused:grok` has its own fixture, in which grok delivers on an `UNVERIFIED` cycle, and it asserts exactly `reason=nongit_root_refused:grok`, with no `no_delivery`. The second-kind members (`truncated`, `vocab_missing:end`, `nongit_root_refused:grok`) each get a fixture that plants that one observation alone, even though no real run produces it alone. Each `input_unparseable` trigger on the grok `Effort:` block gets its own fixture too, asserting `input_unparseable:` followed by the grok `auditcycle.txt` path: a malformed block; the codex-text wrong-file form; the unreadable wrong-file form, planted once for a missing log and once for an empty one; and an unrecognised item. The probe reads the grok log before it reads the item, so these fixtures pair the wrong-file item with a readable grok log. The wrapper never produces that pairing, which makes it synthetic.
  - AC-1.8: **What ships when the smoke fails.** S2 does not start. No calibration runs, no findings are labelled, and no `SEEDSCORE:` verdict exists or is published. (The AC-2.7 main leak check still runs before the measurement doc is committed; a refusal it prints blocks that commit and is not a reading.) What ships:
    - The measurement doc `docs/03-analysis/grok-audit-surface.measurement.md`, holding the `SMOKE: FAIL` line, the AC-1.2 vocabulary table, the AC-1.4 `RESOLVED-MODEL` line and wall time, and the AC-1.6 acceptance outcome per surface.     - `h-mad/references/grok-runtime.md` §"Version and compatibility" gains one sentence: the audit path re-smoke against grok `<version>` failed, where `<version>` is the version the smoke recorded (D4), with the `reason=` values, naming the measurement doc. D3's "'Unmeasured' is retired either way" ranges over the three scored verdicts (PASS, MISS, INCONCLUSIVE), and on a smoke failure there is nothing to retire it with.
    - The AC-6.2 correction of the teammate-section command, and AC-9.5, ship as on every other branch (see "Delivery by outcome").
    - The codex-unavailable route itself (`--surfaces agy,grok`) is not changed by this feature (Out-of-Scope). Whether to stop routing to grok after a failed audit-path smoke is a follow-up, and the measurement doc records it as one.
  - AC-1.9: **Re-smoke under containment** (operator decision 2026-10-20; added in v1.10). The S1 smoke recorded at smoke commit `f7520e72` ran grok with no sandbox, in roots under system temp. It does not stand for the FR-11 configuration. It runs in a new run directory, with roots built as AC-2.6 and AC-11.2 require, with grok under the profile AC-11.1 sets, and after the AC-11.7 session-store quarantine has verified and that run's canary reads `GROK-CANARY: PASS` (AC-11.4).
    - **It is a new reading.** Its `SMOKE:` line is derived from the re-smoke's own records under AC-1.7, and it decides what follows exactly as AC-1.7, AC-1.8 and "Delivery by outcome" state. The `f7520e72` reading is cited in the measurement doc as superseded, and nothing is derived from it again.
    - **Its records.** They are committed on the calibration branch as a later smoke commit (AC-2.7). The S1 records already at `f7520e72` stay byte-identical. The re-smoke adds its records beside them and never overwrites them. The design names the layout and how the smoke component is pointed at the re-smoke's records.

### FR-2: The seeded-defect set
- **Description**: Build the ground truth: N = **27** planted build-class defects across **9** seeded documents, 3 seeds per document. Each document is a copy of one archived, already-audited phase document, and each seed has a label.
- **Source documents (Q2 decision)**: the plan, design and impl-plan of these three archived features, giving 3 × 3 = 9 documents:
  - `docs/archive/2026-09/audit-report-docs-copy/` (small: about 20–37 KB per document; collected legs `codex` + `p1` on all three phases)
  - `docs/archive/2026-09/tdd-gate-fail-opens/` (medium: about 88–109 KB; legs `p1`, `p2`, `agy`)
  - `docs/archive/2026-10/agy-coder-fallback/` (larger: about 92–152 KB; legs `agy`, `p1`, `p2`)

  *Why these three:* each one was gated by at least two collected legs, so its unseeded body has already survived audit, which keeps unplanted findings down. Each has a complete paired set (spec, plan, design), which the assembler requires. Their largest assembled prompt is the agy-coder-fallback design with its paired spec and plan (the assembler pairs a design with the spec and plan, and an impl-plan with the design only; see Premises). Its documents come to 317,110 bytes, under a third of the 1,048,576-char exec ceiling. The bytes are an upper bound on chars, and the archived files are frozen. *Excluded:* `doc-block-exec`, whose documents are around 5,000 lines and whose prompts sit near the ceiling (the brainstorm's reason for deferring corpus replay). Also excluded are features with no paired spec in the archive, because the assembler cannot build their prompts.
- **Why N = 27**: one dispatch per (document, surface) keeps the cost at 9 grok + 9 agy (+ 9 codex) dispatches. 27 seeds give each surface a recall denominator in which a gap of one seed (about 3.7 points) can be published as a count, and four seed classes each appear at least 6 times. A per-phase split (9 seeds per phase) is published for observation only, and no decision reads it (see FR-7).
- **Acceptance Criteria**:
  - AC-2.1: Seed classes. The rule for a seed is that it must be `class: build` under the audit template's test, meaning a 5d/5e implementer's code or tests would differ if the defect were fixed. There are four classes:
    - **C1 contract-token**: a verdict token, reason value, exit code or CLI flag in a contract is changed so that it contradicts the paired spec or another statement of the same contract.
    - **C2 dead-reference**: a `path:symbol`, function or flag the document names is renamed to one that does not exist in the calibration tree.
    - **C3 dropped-failure-mode**: one enumerated failure mode of a contract (an error token, a cannot-judge branch, a refusal) is deleted, so the contract no longer states it.
    - **C4 unsatisfiable-requirement**: an AC is changed so that no implementation can satisfy both it and another AC or enumeration in the same document. This is the 88-vs-94 shape from SKILL.md §"Document-audit round cap".

    The residual is any defect class outside these four, for example measurement-class, prose or ordering defects. None of these is seeded, and the measurement doc says so.
  - AC-2.2: Class distribution. Number the documents 1 to 9 in the order the source list above gives them (feature order, then plan, design, impl-plan). Document k omits class `((k−1) mod 4) + 1` and carries one seed of each of the other three. That yields C1 = 6, C2 = 7, C3 = 7, C4 = 7, which sums to 27. The probe derives these counts from the ledger, never carries them, and refuses a ledger whose counts differ. The check is per document, not only on the totals: for every k, document k's multiset of seed classes must equal exactly the three classes other than `((k−1) mod 4) + 1`, one each. A ledger that meets every document size and every total but gives one document a repeated class (for example d1 as C2, C2, C4 and d4 as C1, C3, C3, which keeps the totals at 6/7/7/7) refuses `ledger_counts`.
  - AC-2.3: Placement. Each seed sits in a distinct `##`/`###` section of its document and outside `## Version History`. No seed lives in the paired documents, which are byte-identical copies of the archive. Each seed's seeded span is unique within its document.
  - AC-2.4: The ledger is committed only on the calibration branch (AC-2.7), under `docs/03-analysis/probes/grok-audit-surface/calibration/` in that branch's tree, as one JSON record per seed with these fields: `id`, `source` (archive path), `phase`, `class` (C1–C4), `origin` (one of the eight `ORIGINS` in `h_mad_audit_origins.py`, naming the real must-fix shape the seed imitates), `section` (heading text), `original` (verbatim pre-seed span), `seeded` (verbatim post-seed span), and `expected` (the finding a correct auditor files, in one sentence). The probe refuses an `origin` outside `ORIGINS`.
  - AC-2.5: The seeded documents are the archive copies with the ledger's edits applied. The probe re-derives every seeded document from `source` + ledger and fails if the result differs from the seeded copy committed on the calibration branch (AC-2.7) by even one byte.
  - AC-2.6: Leak control (operator decision 2026-10-18; replaces the v1.1 worktree rule). The axis is: **any route by which a reviewer with tools, working from the dispatch root, can recover a seed's `original` span or its `expected` finding.** It has three routes, and each one is closed:
    - **(a) This feature's own answer key**: the ledger, the findings, labels and key (the AC-2.7 calibration data, which sits under the excluded probes directory in the calibration branch's tree), and this feature's spec, plan, design, impl-plan and measurement doc.
    - **(b) Git history**: any object store from which `git show <sha>:<archived path>` recovers an original.
    - **(c) Verbatim copies**: `docs/03-analysis/agy-coder-fallback.authoring/`, which quotes the seeded feature's documents, and any other file that copies a seed's original text, named or not.

    The rule over the axis has four parts.
    - **Export root.** Every dispatch (FR-1 and FR-3) runs in a **non-git export root**: a plain directory built from the calibration sha's tree (the AC-2.7 commit on `calibration/grok-audit-surface`; `git archive <sha>` or an equivalent tree export). There is one root per pass: each pass of an S2 cycle runs in its own root, and every root of one cycle is built identically, from the same calibration sha and the same exclusion list, for the same seeded document (AC-3.8; amended in v1.10, which replaced "one root per seeded document"). It has no `.git` entry at any depth, and it shares no object store. It is placed outside every git work tree, so no ancestor repository is reachable. It is never a `git worktree`, a clone, or a checkout. **It is also placed outside system temp** (amended in v1.9). Every root that a dispatch runs in sits under its run's directory, at `$HOME/.local/state/h-mad/grok-audit-surface/<run-dir>/roots/` (`<run-dir>` is defined in AC-11.2), and never under `$TMPDIR`, `/tmp` or `/private/tmp`, nor under any path that resolves into one of them (FR-11, AC-11.2). The reason is measured: grok's `strict` profile grants read and write on all of system temp, so roots placed there can be read and written from one lane to another.
    - **Declared exclusions.** The root builder removes a committed, declared exclusion list before placing any document. The list holds at least each source feature's `docs/archive/*/<feature>/` directory, `docs/03-analysis/probes/grok-audit-surface/`, every path anywhere in the tree whose final component begins with `grok-audit-surface` (this covers `grok-audit-surface-brainstorm.md` as well as the `grok-audit-surface.<phase>.md` files and the measurement doc), and `docs/03-analysis/agy-coder-fallback.authoring/`. The list is published in the measurement doc. It then places the seeded target document, and only the paired documents the assembler reads for that target's phase (Premises), at their live paths.
    - **Content scan, the class closer.** Before any dispatch, the probe reads every regular file in the root, decodes it as UTF-8 with replacement, and collapses every whitespace run to one space. It refuses with `leak:<path>` when a file contains any seed's `original` span or any seed's `expected` sentence, normalised the same way. The scan covers all 27 seeds in every root, not only the target's seeds. There is exactly **one exemption**: a seed's `original` inside the paired copy of that seed's own source document. The design root's paired plan and the impl-plan root's paired design must carry the plan's and the design's seed originals, because AC-2.3 requires them to be byte-identical to the archive. The exemption applies only to a file whose sha256 equals the archived source's, and it never covers `expected`. A hit on the seeded target itself is a `leak:<path>` as well, and it means the seed's `original` span must be widened until it no longer occurs in the seeded document. A scan hit is answered by adding the path to the declared exclusion list or by re-choosing the seed's span, never by an automatic exclusion. Any symlink in the root is a `leak:<path>`, whatever its target. 0 are tracked today (Premises), and the rule does not depend on that zero.
    - **Git reach check.** Before any dispatch, the probe refuses with `root_git:<path>` if the root holds a `.git` entry (file or directory) at any depth, or if `git -C <root> rev-parse --git-dir` succeeds, in which case the path names the repository it found. Every S1 and S2 dispatch command unsets `GIT_DIR`, `GIT_WORK_TREE`, `GIT_COMMON_DIR`, `GIT_OBJECT_DIRECTORY` and `GIT_ALTERNATE_OBJECT_DIRECTORIES`, and the measurement doc records each dispatch command verbatim.

    **Residuals, stated exactly.**
    - (1) A paraphrase, or a copy shorter than the whole span, of a seed's original. The scan matches whole normalised spans only.
    - (2) Content that is not UTF-8 text in its stored form: compressed, base64-encoded or binary files.
    - (3) Reads and **writes** outside the root by absolute path, such as the operator's own checkout or another clone on the same machine (restated in v1.9 and v1.10). This residual was realised twice in the 2026-10-20 S2 run (incident record). A grok pass `cd`-ed to the live checkout and ran `git checkout` there. In 11 of 12 recorded cycles the grok pass's tool calls named the live checkout (Premises), and the incident record shows those calls reading, grepping and listing the unseeded archived originals there, which are the answer key. **The export root bounds what an auditor is shown. It does not bound what the auditor can read or where it can write.** Only an OS sandbox applied to the agent bounds that, and the root, the scan and the reach check do not close it. For measurement validity the bound that matters is the **read** bound: an auditor that can read an original can find every seed by difference. Per surface:
      - **grok:** the strict OS sandbox (FR-11) bounds what it can read and write. Reads are bounded to the working directory, system paths and system temp, and writes to the working directory and system temp (incident record table, not re-run here). Roots outside system temp (AC-11.2) keep other roots out of that bound. The per-run canary (AC-11.4) tests one denied write and one denied read (the read since v1.11). The AC-6.6 scan is the evidence for the read bound on every pass.
      - **codex:** it runs `workspace-write`, whose read bound is not verified. It was never probed against a sibling root or the live checkout. This is an **UNVERIFIED** residual for reads and writes.
      - **agy:** it runs with no sandbox by default, so it has no verified read or write bound. This is also an **UNVERIFIED** residual.

      Neither codex nor agy is claimed to be contained. Moving the roots out of system temp (AC-11.2) removes the shared-temp path for those legs too, and that is still not containment. The AC-6.6 scan publishes, for every standing pass of every surface, what each pass actually named outside its root, and the measurement doc names this residual per surface.
    - (4) Name mentions of a source feature that carry no seed text. These are not a leak, because they recover nothing.
    - (5) Submodule paths, which `git archive` exports empty. A C2 seed must not name a path under the three submodule entries.
  - AC-2.7: **Where calibration data lives** (operator decision 2026-10-18, answering plan audit cycle 2). *Calibration data* means these artifacts, named explicitly:
    - the ledger (AC-2.4) and the seeded copies (AC-2.5);
    - the findings file and the id-to-surface key (AC-4.2), and the labels (AC-4.3);
    - for every S1 and S2 cycle: every pass's report (the report file the agent wrote and the collected copy the scorer reads), every pass log, every kept prompt (the assembled prompt of each pass, which inlines the seeded document), every pass's captured agent output, and the cycle's captured wrapper stdout (`auditcycle.txt`). The wrapper stdout counts because, on a `PASS` or `FAIL` cycle, it prints a `Premise checklist:` carrying the one-line text of every finding of every pass (Premises), and those findings quote seeded spans.

    Any other file that carries a seed's `seeded` span or `expected` sentence, or the text of a finding filed on a seeded document, also counts as calibration data and follows the same rule. A file is outside the set only when it carries none of these; the design names each such file and why (for example the recorded dispatch command), and the catch-all still reaches any of them that turns out to carry such text.
    - **The branch.** Calibration data is committed only on the dedicated branch `calibration/grok-audit-surface`, pushed to origin as `refs/heads/calibration/grok-audit-surface`, under `docs/03-analysis/probes/grok-audit-surface/calibration/` in that branch's tree. **Reading namespace** (v1.15, from design v1.8): each run's records are one reading, under `calibration/runs/<run-id>/` in that directory, where `<run-id>` is the run directory's name. A later run adds a new namespace and never modifies an earlier one. The S1 records committed at `f7520e72`, which predate the namespace, stay where they are (AC-1.9). The branch starts from a `main` commit that already holds the S0 probe. It is never merged into `main` or into any branch that is merged into `main`. It is never rebased, force-pushed or deleted, and archiving this feature on `main` leaves it alone. A later calibration adds commits to it and never rewrites earlier ones.
    - **What `main` holds.** `main` holds the FR-5 probe, its AC-5.5 fixture tests and the FR-6 measurement doc, and no calibration data. Every fixture is synthetic, made of invented text, and never a copy or excerpt of a real seed, ledger record, finding, key or label. The measurement doc cites the calibration data by ref and sha and never quotes it.
    - **Two shas, and the smoke commit.** The *calibration sha* is the branch commit that holds the ledger and the seeded copies. Every AC-2.6 export root is built from it before any dispatch, the FR-1 smoke included. The *smoke commit* is a later branch commit, which adds the S1 cycle records (each S1 cycle's calibration data listed above, its captured wrapper stdout with its `AUDITCYCLE:` line, and the record of the root it ran in), under the calibration directory. It is made on every smoke outcome that derives to `PASS` or `FAIL`. When S1 is run again (AC-1.9), the smoke commit a reading cites is the one that adds the re-smoke's records. It descends from the earlier smoke commit `f7520e72`, whose S1 records stay byte-identical (amended in v1.10; until v1.9 this sentence read "the next branch commit"). The *score sha* is the later branch commit, descending from the smoke commit, that adds the findings file, the key and the labels, and every FR-3 cycle's records, including its captured wrapper stdout and so its recorded `AUDITCYCLE:` line (the wrapper's stdout token line for that cycle, which AC-3.2 and AC-6.4 read), all under the calibration directory. Both the smoke commit and the score sha must descend from the calibration sha and differ from it only under `docs/03-analysis/probes/grok-audit-surface/calibration/`, so an export root rebuilt from any of them is the same root. Within that directory, later commits only add: every file present at the calibration sha (the ledger and every seeded copy) is byte-identical at the smoke commit and at the score sha, so the ground truth the roots were built from is the ground truth that is scored. The probe refuses with `calibration_drift:<path>` when a path outside that directory differs, or when a file present at the calibration sha differs or is absent at the later commit, naming the first such path in sorted order, and with `calibration_drift:not_descendant` when the later commit does not descend from the calibration sha, or when the score sha does not descend from the smoke commit (the score sha is required to descend from the smoke commit, not only from the calibration sha). A smoke failure (AC-1.8) produces no score sha; the smoke commit is then the branch tip, and it is cited.
    - **Main leak check.** The probe scans every blob in the tree of the commit checked out where it runs (`HEAD`, read through `git ls-tree -r`, never the work tree). The commit it scanned is printed and recorded in the measurement doc. It refuses with `main_leak:<path>` on any one of three triggers: (i) the blob's sha256 equals that of a calibration data file at the score sha, or, when there is no score sha (a smoke failure), at the smoke commit; (ii) after AC-2.6 whitespace normalisation, the blob contains any seed's `expected` sentence; (iii) the blob contains any seed's normalised `seeded` span, unless that span also occurs in the normalised archived source document of the same seed. Residuals, stated exactly: (1) a `seeded` span that also occurs in its own archived source, which (iii) cannot tell apart from archive text; (2) a partial copy of a calibration data file that carries no whole `seeded` span and no whole `expected` sentence; (3) content that is not UTF-8 text in its stored form, as in AC-2.6 residual (2); (4) uncommitted files in the work tree and commits other than the scanned one; (5) calibration objects fetched into a local clone's object store, which are not in `main`'s tree and which the non-git export roots cannot reach (AC-2.6 route (b)). **When it runs**: on every outcome that publishes a measurement doc, before that doc is committed to `main`, and again inside the AC-6.4 re-run. On a smoke failure there is no score sha and no re-run, so the check runs against the smoke commit's calibration data, by a probe path that needs no score sha, and the measurement doc records the commit it scanned exactly as on the other outcomes. On any outcome, a `main_leak:<path>` refusal blocks the commit of the measurement doc to `main`; the leak is removed from `main` and the check is run again. A refusal is never published as a reading.
    - **Ref failure modes.** The probe reaches calibration data only through the ref. It refuses with `calibration_ref:<kind>`, and `<kind>` takes exactly one of four values:
      - `fetch_failed`: `git ls-remote` or the fetch of `refs/heads/calibration/grok-audit-surface` from origin did not complete (network, authentication, or no `origin` remote).
      - `ref_absent`: origin answered, but it has no such ref.
      - `sha_unknown:<sha>`: after the fetch, a cited sha does not resolve to a commit.
      - `not_on_ref:<sha>`: a cited sha is neither the fetched ref's tip nor an ancestor of it, which means the branch was rewritten.

      There is no other kind. On any of them, no reading is reproduced and the published reading stays unverified. The operator restores the ref, and never edits the measurement doc's shas to make them fit.

### FR-3: Calibration dispatch through the normal audit-cycle path
- **Description**: Each seeded document is audited once per surface, in one cycle, through `hmad-dispatch audit-cycle`. With codex available the call is `--passes 3 --surfaces codex,agy,grok`, which is the same shape as a production first gate. With codex unavailable it is `--passes 2 --surfaces agy,grok`. Each pass runs in its own AC-2.6 export root for the seeded document (AC-3.8), never in a git work tree, and every NDJSON log is kept.

  **Which S2 run is scored** (operator decision 2026-10-20, route (a); amended in v1.10). The S2 run that is scored is a fresh one, made after FR-11 and AC-3.9 ship, after the AC-1.9 re-smoke reads `SMOKE: PASS`, and after the second AC-11.7 quarantine verifies. It covers all nine documents on all three surfaces (or the codex-unavailable shape), with every document's first cycle numbered `v1`, in the same new run directory as the re-smoke. The S2 run of 2026-10-20 (`run-ab2c85dc2e25/data/s2/` in the kept directory) is incident evidence only, because its grok passes named the live checkout in 11 of 12 cycles (Premises). It is never scored, and none of its readings is published as a measurement. It is never committed under the calibration directory at a path the probe reads as an S2 cycle record. If it is preserved on the branch at all, the design names a path that no probe mode reads as a cycle. The two halted Task 15 runs of 2026-10-22 (`docs/03-analysis/grok-audit-surface.incident-2026-10-22-task15-halts.md`) are not readings either: neither reached S2, neither was scored, and neither ran with the AC-3.9 clause. Their data is evidence only, under the same rules. No `SEEDSCORE:` line and no measurement doc exist yet, so the clause invalidates no scored result (added in v1.17).
- **Acceptance Criteria**:
  - AC-3.1: Every assembly reads `ASSEMBLE: PASS`. Any other token halts the run for that document and is published, never skipped silently.
  - AC-3.2: The prompts are identical across surfaces. Within one cycle, the per-pass prompts may differ **only in lines that contain a per-pass report path**. **The wrapper is the single authority for this check** (single-source contract): `_cmd_audit_cycle` in `h-mad/scripts/hmad-dispatch.sh`, before any dispatch, diffs pass 1's prompt against each later pass, and requires at least one differing line with every differing line containing, as a fixed string (`grep -F`), one of the two passes' report paths. An empty diff fails, and so does any other differing line; on either it halts the cycle with `AUDITCYCLE: UNVERIFIED reason=prompt_divergence` and dispatches no agent. **Sentinel lines are not masked**: the wrapper passes no `--sentinel`, so every pass in one cycle carries the same `AUDIT-<feature>-<phase>-v<cycle>`, and a sentinel difference is a real divergence. The comparison is within one cycle only, because a re-dispatched cycle (AC-3.4) has a new cycle number and therefore a new sentinel. **The probe does not diff prompts and carries no report-path mask.** It decides AC-3.2 per cycle from that cycle's recorded `AUDITCYCLE:` line (AC-2.7) alone: a `reason=prompt_divergence` line fails the cycle, which is published and goes through AC-3.4, and a cycle that reached dispatch passed the wrapper's check. A cycle with no recorded `AUDITCYCLE:` line, or one that does not parse, is never read as passing (AC-5.4 `cycle_token:<cycle>`).
  - AC-3.3: Every pass keeps its pass log, and the measurement doc records each log's `log_sha256` from the pass's `.effort.json` sidecar.
  - AC-3.4: Re-dispatch rule (the v1.9 void-cycle amendment is withdrawn in v1.10). A cycle whose `AUDITCYCLE:` is `UNVERIFIED` (any `reason=`) is re-dispatched once as a fresh cycle number. The second outcome stands. A pass whose report was never delivered (`no_report`, `unscorable_report`, or `delivered=none`) counts as zero findings, so its seeds are misses for that surface. A delivered report is scored even when its pass read `low_evidence`, and the measurement doc publishes per-surface delivery and evidence outcomes as counts (`delivered=<n>` and `low_evidence=<n>` on each surface line). Evidence has one non-count value: when a scored cycle's recorded `Effort:` block does not parse (the AC-1.7 malformed-block shapes), every surface that cycle ran publishes `low_evidence=unreadable` instead of a count. An absent `Effort:` block counts zero and is not unreadable. `unreadable` is observational: it is never a refusal, it adds no AC-5.4 reason, and it never changes the `SEEDSCORE:` verdict (orchestrator decision on implementation Task 9, option (b)).
  - AC-3.5: `--timeout` is the FR-1 derived value, given explicitly, and is the same for every pass.
  - AC-3.6: The calibration ref `refs/heads/calibration/grok-audit-surface`, the full 40-hex calibration sha, smoke commit and score sha (AC-2.7), the full 40-hex sha of the exclusion-list commit (the `main` commit whose committed AC-2.6 declared exclusion list every S1 and S2 export root was built with) together with that list's blob id, the grok, agy and codex versions, and each surface's `RESOLVED-MODEL` line are recorded once in the measurement doc.     - **The assembler commit** (added in v1.17; amended in v1.20). AC-3.9 changes the calibration prompt, so each run records the h-mad commit its prompts were assembled under. It records the full 40-hex sha of the commit checked out in the h-mad directory, whether `h-mad/` had uncommitted changes there, and that directory itself. The sha and dirtiness are taken in two run-level readings. The directory is recorded in two ways. Each reading names a directory. The first reading is taken before the first re-smoke cycle starts, after the tracer pair and the canary. The second is taken when the run's last S2 cycle ends, or, on a smoke failure that leaves no S2, after the last re-smoke cycle (design D21). The run is **pinned** only when both readings give one sha, the tree is clean at both, `HMAD_AUDIT_CYCLE_SCRIPT_DIR` was not set, and the commit carries the AC-3.9 clause. Every other case is not pinned. That covers a dirty `h-mad/` tree, an unrecorded or unreadable sha, two different shas, and a set `HMAD_AUDIT_CYCLE_SCRIPT_DIR`, which are `ASSEMBLER: UNPINNED`, and a commit without the clause, which is `ASSEMBLER: NO_CLAUSE`. The exact, ordered reason lists of both tokens are single-sourced in design D21, and every reason on them is an instance of "every other case" (v1.19).       - **A run that is not pinned is not scored** (operator ruling 2026-10-22; v1.18). It is not a reading: no `extract`, no labels, no score sha. It is recorded as an incident on `main` (Delivery by outcome), and the remedy is a new run on a clean commit that carries the clause.

The incident record carries the `ASSEMBLER: UNPINNED` or `ASSEMBLER: NO_CLAUSE` token line and the readings verbatim. A path-scope halt carries no `ASSEMBLER:` token, only the `path_scope_missing` line. No `assembler=unpinned` stamp is written anywhere (v1.21). The measurement doc exists only for a pinned run, and its stamp is always `assembler=<sha40>`.
      - **Enforcement** (v1.20, discharged by design D21). The runbook gate reads the `ASSEMBLER:` token with `require --verb assembler`, and only `ASSEMBLER: PINNED` gives `REQUIRE: OK`. The gate runs before `extract` and `score`. No probe check and no AC-5.4 reason is added, so the sixteen reason forms, the AC-5.5 fixtures and the `RUN:` keys of `list_changed` trigger (c) (`checkout`, `home`) are unchanged. Residual: the probe itself does not read the pin, so an AC-6.4 re-score of a reading cannot re-check it. A measurement doc that carries an `assembler=<sha40>` stamp is the evidence that the gate passed.
  - AC-3.7: Retired in v1.10. In v1.9 it defined operator-declared void cycles for route (b). Route (a) replaces that route (FR-3, "Which S2 run is scored"), so no cycle is ever void, and no probe mode reads a void declaration.
  - AC-3.8: **Each pass of a cycle runs in its own export root** (operator decision 2026-10-20; added in v1.10). Under `strict`, a grok pass writes its report inside its own working directory (AC-11.3). With one root shared by a cycle's parallel passes, another pass of the same cycle could read that report while it was being written. Separate roots close that route.
    - **The rule.** Every pass of an S2 cycle runs in a root of its own: a fresh AC-2.6 export root for that cycle's seeded document. No pass's root is any other pass's root, or lies inside it. The roots of one cycle are built identically, from the same calibration sha and the same exclusion-list commit and blob. Each passes the AC-2.6 content scan and git reach check before any dispatch of the cycle. Each root has its own root record (`ROOT: READY`), which goes to the branch with the cycle. The design chooses the layout, and how `audit-cycle` is given one root per pass.

With per-pass roots, a pass's report path lies inside its root (design D17), so from then on the premise reads "no root path outside the report-path lines", which AC-3.2 already permits (v1.19). The assembler and its default template are unchanged from there to `242c26bd`: `git log --oneline a489031f..242c26bd -- h-mad/scripts/h_mad_assemble_audit.py h-mad/audit-prompt.template.md` lists 0 commits. The assembled prompt said nothing about whose paths those are (`docs/03-analysis/grok-audit-surface.incident-2026-10-22-task15-halts.md` §"Ruling, 2026-10-22: route (b)", committed at `242c26bd`; the rejected routes (a) and (c) and their reasons are recorded there). (i) A path written inside the document under review, or inside any document inlined in the prompt, refers to that document's author's environment. The design chooses the wording and where the clause sits. Design D21 chose the form: the wrapper halts the cycle before any dispatch and prints `REQUIRE: STOP reason=path_scope_missing:p<i>`, which the runbook reads as a token, never from `$?`. No AC-5.4 reason is added (v1.20). A switch that cannot be set, or that the assembler does not recognise, has the same outcome: the cycle is not scored. It gives a different signal, though. The assembler exits 2 with no prompt, so the wrapper exits 4 with no `AUDITCYCLE:` line, no `REQUIRE: STOP` and no halt-file line, and `record-cycle` refuses the cycle (design D21; v1.21). It is never a silent fallback to the production prompt.

### FR-4: Finding classification and fabrication confirmation (Q3 decision)
- **Description**: Every must-fix is classified **by hand**, against the calibration tree and the assembled prompt, with the reviewer surface hidden from whoever labels it. Mechanical checks do two things only: they extract findings, and they propose candidates. They never decide a label. *Why:* `docs/03-analysis/hmad-audit-evidence-gate.measurement.md` measured the best automatic span rule catching 4 of 6 fabrications while killing 13 of 31 real must-fixes, and it found two fabrications that no occurrence check can see: one quotes nothing, and one paraphrases.
- **Acceptance Criteria**:
  - AC-4.1: Extraction uses the gate's own machinery (`h_mad_audit_gate.classify_detail`, or the same section walk). For every report, the probe asserts that its extracted must-fix count equals `classify(text)["must_count"]`. A mismatch makes the run `UNREADABLE`.
  - AC-4.2: Blinding. The probe writes every must-fix to a findings file under an opaque id that does not encode the surface, in a shuffled order, and writes the id-to-surface key to a separate file. The labeler works from the findings file alone. The findings file, the key and the labels are calibration data: they are committed only on the calibration branch, at the score sha (AC-2.7).
  - AC-4.3: Each must-fix gets exactly one label:
    - `HIT:<seed-id>`: the finding locates the seed's section, or quotes inside its `seeded` span, AND it states the seed's `expected` defect.
    - `REAL`: its premise holds against source, and it is not a seed.
    - `FABRICATED`: its premise fails against the calibration tree or the assembled prompt.
    - `UNDECIDABLE`: the labeler cannot settle it, and records one sentence saying why.

    Any other label value makes the run `UNREADABLE`.
  - AC-4.4: Candidate hints are advisory only. For a `quote:` line whose span is absent from the file it names, the probe may print a `candidate-fabrication` hint, and for a `quote:` span overlapping a seed's `seeded` span it may print a `candidate-hit:<seed-id>` hint. Hints are printed beside a finding and never written as labels. A test asserts that changing or removing every hint leaves the score unchanged.
  - AC-4.5: Counting rules. A seed hit by several findings from one surface counts once toward that surface's recall. One finding can hit at most one seed. Recall counts must-fixes only, because the gate is on must-fixes. Should-fix hits are published separately and never enter the bar.

### FR-5: The scorer probe
- **Description**: A committed, stdlib-only probe under `docs/03-analysis/probes/grok-audit-surface/` that builds the seeded documents, checks leak control, extracts and blinds findings, and computes the verdict from the ledger, the labels and the key. The probe lives on `main`; the data it scores lives only on the calibration branch (AC-2.7). It is tested in the suite.
- **Acceptance Criteria**:
  - AC-5.1: The probe's score ends on one token line: `SEEDSCORE: PASS|MISS|INCONCLUSIVE|UNREADABLE`, with `reason=` on every verdict other than PASS. The reason values are closed: MISS carries `reason=bar_missed` and no other; INCONCLUSIVE carries exactly one of `reason=seeds_undetected` or `reason=undecidable_flips` (AC-5.3); UNREADABLE carries exactly one AC-5.4 reason. It exits 0 on PASS, MISS or INCONCLUSIVE and 2 on UNREADABLE, and callers read the token, never `$?`. **Every exit carries a final token.** Every invocation of the probe, in every mode and on every path out of it (an exception that escapes the mode's own checks included), ends on exactly one token line on stdout; there is no exit without one. An unexpected failure that no listed reason names is `SEEDSCORE: UNREADABLE reason=internal:<ExceptionName>` (AC-5.4) and exits 2, and its traceback goes to stderr, never to stdout. A **usage error** is declared, not unexpected. It is every error the argument parser reports, for example an unrecognised flag, an abbreviated flag, `--help` (which the probe does not offer), a missing required argument, or an argument value that its type or `choices` rejects, together with the probe's own check of a flag given without its required partner. It prints the named mode's own token with `internal:UsageError` (the smoke prints `input_unparseable:` with its `--data` value, AC-1.7), writes its usage message to stderr with no traceback, and exits 2. **No mode named.** An invocation whose argv is empty, or whose first word is not one of the probe's nine modes (`check-ledger`, `build-root`, `record-cycle`, `commit-data`, `smoke`, `extract`, `score`, `rescore`, `main-leak`), has no mode grammar. It prints exactly one stdout line, `PROBE: UNREADABLE reason=unknown_mode`, and nothing else on stdout. Usage and an `[H-MAD]` marker go to stderr, and it exits 2. `PROBE:` belongs to no mode, `UNREADABLE` is its only verdict, and `unknown_mode` is its only reason. Residual: a first word that is a mode name, followed by arguments the mode does not take, is that mode's usage error, never `unknown_mode`. The smoke's token is `SMOKE:` (AC-1.7), whose unexpected failures map to its own two UNREADABLE reasons. The literal `SEEDSCORE: UNREADABLE reason=internal:<ExceptionName>` is the form for the score and rescore modes only; every other mode except the smoke carries `internal:<ExceptionName>` inside its own token grammar, which the design's per-mode token-grammar table names, so no mode prints two grammars.
  - AC-5.2: The fields per surface are `recall=<hits>/27`, `musts=<n>`, `fabricated=<f>`, `undecidable=<u>` and `fab_rate=<f>/<n−u>`, each written as an exact fraction. A surface with no decidable must-fix (`n − u = 0`) prints `fab_rate=0/0`, which compares as zero fabrications. The surface line also carries the AC-3.4 observational fields `delivered=<n>` and `low_evidence=<n>` (or `low_evidence=unreadable`), which no verdict rule reads.
  - AC-5.3: Verdict rules are computed with exact rational comparison and no rounding. "D3 holds" means the amended D3 predicate is true: `(fab_grok < fab_agy AND recall_grok ≥ recall_agy) OR (fab_grok = fab_agy AND recall_grok > recall_agy)`. Equality of fabrication rates is equality of the reduced rationals (`0/0` compares as zero, AC-5.2), so `1/4` and `2/8` are equal. A full tie on both quantities makes the predicate false.
    - **PASS**: D3 holds when every one of grok's UNDECIDABLE is counted as fabricated and every one of agy's is counted as not fabricated.
    - **MISS** (`reason=bar_missed`): D3 fails when the opposite worst case is taken (grok's undecidables counted as not fabricated, agy's as fabricated).
    - **INCONCLUSIVE**: covers every other case, with exactly one of two reasons. `reason=seeds_undetected` applies when no surface, codex included, hit any seed, which means the seeds measure nothing. `reason=undecidable_flips` applies to every remaining case, in which the two worst cases disagree.

    **Precedence.** The verdict is decided in this order, and the first rule that applies wins: (1) `seeds_undetected`; (2) PASS; (3) MISS (`reason=bar_missed`); (4) `undecidable_flips`. **`seeds_undetected` outranks PASS and MISS**: when no surface hit any seed, the verdict is `INCONCLUSIVE reason=seeds_undetected` even when the PASS rule or the MISS rule also holds. For example, zero hits on every surface with grok's pessimistic fabrication rate strictly below agy's optimistic one satisfies the PASS rule (`0 ≥ 0` on recall), and it scores `INCONCLUSIVE reason=seeds_undetected`, never `PASS`.
  - AC-5.4: UNREADABLE reasons, all fail-closed. **The list is closed**: these sixteen reason forms are the only ones, and each names every trigger it covers.
    - `ledger_counts` (AC-2.2): the per-document class rule or a derived total fails; or a ledger line does not parse as one JSON object, or a record lacks one of the nine AC-2.4 fields; or a record's `source` is not one of the nine FR-2 documents (which also keeps every seed out of the paired documents, AC-2.3); or a seed's `original` or `expected` is empty (an empty needle would match every file).
    - `ledger_origin` (AC-2.4): a record's `origin` is outside `ORIGINS`.
    - `seed_rebuild` (AC-2.5, AC-2.3): the re-derived seeded document differs from the committed seeded copy by any byte; or a seed's `original` does not occur exactly once in its archived source; or an **AC-2.3 placement violation**: two seeds of one document in the same `##`/`###` section, a seed under `## Version History`, a record whose `section` does not name the heading its span sits under, or a `seeded` span that does not occur exactly once in its seeded document.

    - `must_count_mismatch:<report>` (AC-4.1).
    - `label:<id>` (AC-4.3): a finding with no label; two or more label records for one id, whether identical or conflicting; a label for an id not in the findings file; a value outside the four AC-4.3 labels; an `UNDECIDABLE` with no reason sentence; or a `HIT` naming a seed that is unknown or that belongs to another document than the finding's.

    - `calibration_drift:<path>` and `calibration_drift:not_descendant` (AC-2.7 "Two shas, and the smoke commit"). `not_descendant` covers both descent failures: the smoke commit or the score sha does not descend from the calibration sha, or the score sha does not descend from the smoke commit.
    - `main_leak:<path>` (AC-2.7 main leak check).
    - `calibration_ref:<kind>`, where `<kind>` is `fetch_failed`, `ref_absent`, `sha_unknown:<sha>` or `not_on_ref:<sha>` (AC-2.7 ref failure modes).
    - `cycle_token:<cycle>` (AC-3.2, AC-3.4, AC-3.8): a cycle record that the score cannot read as written. `<cycle>` is `<doc>.v<N>`. The triggers are exactly eight. (1)–(7) are the pre-v1.9 seven, restored in v1.10 when the v1.9 void triggers were withdrawn, and (8) is added in v1.10 for AC-3.8:
      - (1) the cycle has no recorded `AUDITCYCLE:` line at the score sha;

A missing line is UNREADABLE, never PASS, and is never read as AC-3.4 `UNVERIFIED`, because a re-score cannot tell "never recorded" from "re-dispatched".

That means it holds no such line, or more than one, or one that does not carry exactly the root record's seven fields (`doc`, `purpose`, `path`, `calibration`, `list_commit`, `list_blob`, `absent`), each once with a non-empty value. (c) the run record `s2/run.txt` of the reading (AC-6.6's live-checkout record) is absent, or does not yield exactly one parseable `RUN:` line with exactly the keys `checkout` and `home`. A record that names no list blob is a case of (b). A malformed record is never skipped. A re-run never rebuilds roots with a list other than the one the dispatches used.
    - `out_of_root:<doc>.v<N>` (AC-6.6; added in v1.10): a grok pass of a recorded S2 cycle failed the AC-6.6 containment scan. The scan is re-derived from that pass's recorded log, and the reason applies to every recorded cycle, whether it stands or was re-dispatched. The triggers are exactly two: (1) the scan's contamination count, `checkout`, for the grok pass is non-zero (v1.23; before v1.23 this was the out-of-root count, and an `OUT_OF_ROOT` scan with `checkout=0` is now published, never refused); (2) the grok pass's log is absent or unreadable, or yields no parseable event, so the scan cannot be run. A codex or agy count never triggers it (AC-6.6). The first such cycle in document and cycle-number order is named.

An expected I/O or parse failure inside a listed check is reported as that check's reason, never as `internal`; `internal` is the last resort, so that no exit lacks a token. A usage error is `internal:UsageError` (AC-5.1).

    Sixteen counts the reason forms above, with `calibration_drift`'s two forms as two and each parameterised form (`leak`, `root_git`, `must_count_mismatch`, `label`, `main_leak`, `calibration_ref`, `cycle_token`, `list_changed`, `out_of_root`, `internal`) as one. `PROBE: UNREADABLE reason=unknown_mode` (AC-5.1) is not among them.       A path counts when the call spells it as an absolute path, or as a path beginning `~/`, `$HOME/` or `${HOME}/`. Every spelling is normalised to its absolute form, with `$HOME` taken as the run's home directory, before it is classified. Each base a path is compared with (the root, the live checkout, `$HOME` and each needle directory) is normalised once, the same lexical way: dot components and repeated slashes fold, a leading `//` becomes `/`, and the trailing `/` is dropped, so `/x/r/`, `/x//r` and `/x/./r` all mean `/x/r`. (1) A synthetic fixture on `main` (AC-5.5): a grok log whose out-of-root `tool_call` comes after a non-JSON line must count at least 1. (2) Before the fresh S2's scan is published, the shipped scan runs on the incident's kept `run-ab2c85dc2e25/data/s2/d5/v1/p3.log`, outside git, and its `checkout=` count must be non-zero. The incident's own predicate reads 14 tool calls there (Premises). The control's line is published with the scan. A control that reads 0 makes the scan invalid: no scan line of that run is published, and the scan is repaired and run again.
    - **The halt rule for grok.** grok runs under `strict` in every calibration dispatch (AC-11.1). A grok pass with `checkout` > 0 (contamination, v1.23), or whose log cannot be scanned (absent, unreadable, or no parseable event), is a **containment failure**. A grok pass with `out_of_root` > 0 and `checkout` = 0 is not a containment failure: its scan reads `OUT_OF_ROOT`, is published, and neither halts the run nor refuses the score. That is the operator's 2026-10-24 ruling on run 7, whose only out path was `/tmp` from a shell `HOME=/tmp`. `/tmp` is not exempted, because Claude scratchpads live under `/private/tmp/claude-501/`. The scan runs on each grok pass once its cycle ends, before the run dispatches another grok leg. On a containment failure the run halts. No further cycle of that run is dispatched (cycles already in flight finish and are recorded), the run is not scored, and the halt is recorded as an incident on `main`. This covers the AC-1.9 re-smoke's grok pass and every S2 grok pass, standing or not. An exempt call (above) never halts. The AC-11.4 canary is not a pass: it is asked to write and read outside its working directory, it is judged by its own token, and it is not scanned. The probe enforces the same rule on recorded data (AC-5.4 `out_of_root:<doc>.v<N>`). The design names the verb that runs the scan at cycle end and its token. Callers read the token, never `$?`. **Token consumer** (orchestrator ruling, v1.15): every `GROK-CANARY:`, `QUARANTINE:` and `TOOLSCAN:` token the runbook gates on is read by `h_mad_grok_containment.py require`, which prints `REQUIRE: OK` or `REQUIRE: STOP` and writes the halt file on a toolscan STOP. The `SMOKE:` and `SEEDSCORE:` gates are not among them; they are read from the probe's and the smoke component's own token lines (Delivery by outcome).
    - **Why the grok exemption is narrow, and its residual, stated exactly** (v1.12, which replaced v1.11's whole-store exemption).
      - **The measured fact.** grok's session store under `$HOME/.grok/sessions/` is shared by every grok session on the machine. Each session directory is keyed by its working directory and keeps that session's full prompt. A design audit's prompt inlines the byte-identical archived plan, and an impl-plan audit's prompt inlines the archived design (Premises, v1.2). Those paired copies are the **unseeded originals** of other calibration targets. This was measured, not ruled: in each of the 14 `gas-root-` session directories left from the S1 smoke and the 2026-10-20 S2 run, every ledger seed's `original` was whitespace-normalised and searched for in the concatenated prompt files (Premises, v1.11). For 6 of the 9 ledger sources (the three plans and the three designs), all three seeds' originals occur in at least one of those directories. For the three impl-plans they occur in none. A grok pass that reads another session's prompt can therefore be shown the original of the document it audits, and can find its seeds by difference. v1.11 had recorded an operator reason that said the opposite. This revision withdraws that reason on this measurement (operator ruling 2026-10-20).
      - **What the old run's passes did.** Their `$HOME/.grok/` calls named their own session directory or `$HOME/.grok/skills/`, never another session's directory (Premises). The two grok exemptions above are exactly that behaviour.
      - **Residual (1).** A pass's own session directory holds its own prompt: its seeded target and the paired copies of its own root, which are already in its root. Reading it shows nothing the root does not. If grok keys a session by a spelling of the working directory other than the recorded root path, the pass's own-session calls count in `out_of_root` and, as another session's path, in `checkout`, so they halt the run (v1.23). That fails closed.
      - **Residual (2).** `$HOME/.grok/skills/` is not inspected for calibration data. It is exempt because grok's own skill files live there and the old run read nothing else in it (Premises).
      - **Residual (3).** Whether `strict` refuses reads of other sessions' directories is not measured, so this spec treats them as readable. The scan sees a read of one only when a tool call names it, and then it halts the run (AC-6.6 residual (1) covers spellings it cannot see). The quarantine (AC-11.7) removes earlier runs' sessions before the run. It does not remove the sessions the run's own cycles create, and the narrowed exemption, not the quarantine, is what covers those.
    - **codex and agy.** A non-zero count is a published finding on that pass, never a halt and never a refusal. A standing pass with `checkout=` > 0 is named in the measurement doc as one that could have read the answer key.
    - **Residuals, stated exactly.**
      - (1) A path that the call's arguments do not spell in one of the four forms above: one built at run time (shell variables other than `$HOME`, command substitution) or reached by relative `..` traversal out of the root.
      - (2) codex MCP calls, which are counted only as `mcp_unmeasured`, never scanned.
      - (3) Reads that an agent's own runtime performs outside any tool call (its configuration and session loading).
      - (4) A surface whose log format changes so that the parser finds no tool calls. For grok this is a containment failure (no parseable event). For codex and agy a scan that finds no tool calls in a pass that delivered a report is published as `unmeasured`, never as 0.
    - **Tests.** The cycle-end verb's every token value gets its own fixture on a synthetic log, each alone: a clean grok log, each `out_of_root` trigger of AC-5.4, and the non-JSON-line positive-control shape. A halt fixture asserts that no further cycle is dispatched. The probe's side is in AC-5.5.

### FR-11: Containment of grok dispatch writes (operator decision 2026-10-20; added in v1.9)
- **Description**: S2 cycle `s2-d5-v1` showed that an export root bounds what an auditor is shown, not where it can write: a grok pass with a shell tool rewrote the operator's live checkout (incident record). The operator chose an agent-side OS sandbox, grok's `--sandbox strict`, with four supporting measures:
  - the roots move out of system temp;
  - grok's prompt and temp files stay inside its root;
  - nothing seeded is written to system temp;
  - a per-run canary that fails closed.

  v1.12 adds a fifth measure, by operator ruling 2026-10-20: a verified quarantine of earlier runs' grok sessions (AC-11.7). The FR states what each measure guarantees and what it does not. **Gating:** FR-11 ships, and its tests are green, before any further grok dispatch against a seeded document. That covers the AC-1.9 re-smoke, the fresh S2 run (FR-3, "Which S2 run is scored"), and any later calibration (amended in v1.10, which withdrew the v1.9 route (b) dispatches). Task 17 (S4 routing, FR-7) stays forbidden until FR-11 ships, whatever the `SEEDSCORE:` verdict.
- **Acceptance Criteria**:
  - AC-11.1: **grok's profile defaults to `strict`.** The rule ranges over every `hmad-dispatch` verb that launches a grok process. At `e94727ce` those are `exec` and `audit-cycle` through `exec` (Premises); `exec-pane` refuses grok.
    - `exec grok` with no `--sandbox` passes `--sandbox strict` to grok, and so does `exec grok --sandbox strict`.
    - Any other `--sandbox` value is a weaker or unknown profile. `exec` refuses it with exit 2 and a usage message, launches nothing, and prints the refusal on stderr, unless the caller also gives the explicit override flag (the design names it). With the flag, grok runs under the given profile.
    - grok's profile is selected through three channels: the `--sandbox` flag, the inherited `GROK_SANDBOX` variable (Premises), and grok's own configuration files. The first two are closed: `exec` always passes `--sandbox` explicitly for grok and removes `GROK_SANDBOX` from the grok child's environment. Residual: a profile set in grok's configuration files is not inspected. The canary (AC-11.4) is what detects a profile that does not deny.
    - Every grok launch writes its effective profile, and whether it was the default or an override, into a file that `record-cycle` copies with the cycle. The design names the file and the line, so each pass's profile can be derived from the cycle record (AC-6.5). A pass whose record names no profile reads as `none`, never as `strict`.
    - `audit-cycle` passes no profile to `exec` at `e94727ce`, so every audit-cycle grok leg runs `strict`. If the design adds a pass-through, the override appears verbatim in the recorded dispatch command.
    - `exec-pane` either keeps refusing grok (`unknown agent 'grok' (expected codex|agy)`, exit 2) or, if it ever admits grok, gets the same default through the inner `exec` it runs. A pin test asserts one of the two.
    - Residual: a grok process started outside `hmad-dispatch`, such as an interactive or host-mode session, is not covered.
  - AC-11.2: **No calibration data in system temp.**
    - **Roots.** The rule is over two axes: *every root a dispatch runs in*, and *every file, written by the probe or by the wrapper during a calibration run, that carries calibration data (the AC-2.7 definition: seeded text, ledger fields, prompts, agent output, reports or findings)*. Neither goes under `$TMPDIR`, `/tmp`, `/private/tmp`, or any path that resolves into one of them. Dispatch roots are created under the run directory's `roots/`, at `$HOME/.local/state/h-mad/grok-audit-surface/<run-dir>/roots/`, which is outside every git work tree (Premises) and outside system temp. `<run-dir>` is the run's own directory. For the AC-1.9 re-smoke and the fresh S2 it is a new directory, never `run-ab2c85dc2e25`, and the design names its form (amended in v1.10; v1.9 wrote `run-<cal12>`, which with the unchanged calibration sha names the 2026-10-20 run's directory).
    - **Members at `e94727ce`, each of which moves or is shown not to carry calibration data.** In the probe:
      - the `gas-root-` call, covering dispatch roots, rescore roots and the S1 reset's expected tree;
      - `gas-ledger-` (both sites);
      - `gas-extract-`;
      - `gas-main-leak-`;
      - `gas-rescore-`.

      `gas-commit-` holds a git index file only, so it may stay if the design confirms that. In the wrapper:
      - the grok prompt from `mktemp -t hmad_exec_prompt.XXXXXX` (AC-11.3);
      - codex's last-message file from `mktemp -t hmad_exec_last.XXXXXX`, which holds codex's findings;
      - the `exec` default log from `mktemp -t hmad_exec_log.XXXXXX`, used when no `--log` is given;
      - the `audit-cycle` stem dir default `/tmp`, used when `HMAD_AUDIT_STEM_DIR` is unset.

      The wrapper's other six system-temp `mktemp` sites at `a489031f` (Premises: `_cmd_env`, `_cmd_pin_agents`, `_cmd_pin`, `_cmd_probe`, `_exec_wt_target`, `_cmd_exec_pane`) are outside the class. Five carry no calibration data by content, and `_cmd_exec_pane`'s default log is not on the calibration path, because `exec-pane` refuses grok and the runbook does not use it for codex or agy. If the runbook ever uses `exec-pane`, its log joins the class and is given a `--log` under the run directory (amended in v1.10; v1.9 listed only three wrapper sites).

      The calibration runbook already supplies `--log` and `HMAD_AUDIT_STEM_DIR` under the run directory (design D8). For those two members, the requirement is that the runbook keeps doing so and that the probe or the runbook refuses a calibration dispatch that would fall back to the default. The design states, for codex's last-message file, where it goes and whether codex can write it there; if codex cannot, the design reports that back to this spec rather than leaving it in system temp.
    - **Guards that assume system temp.** `check_reset_target`'s `ResetTargetOutsideTempdir` (`# M:GAS-RESET-TMPDIR`), which requires the target's parent to be `tempfile.gettempdir()`, is re-anchored to the run's `roots/` directory. The `gas-root-<doc>-s1-` prefix guard (`ResetTargetPrefix`) and its siblings keep their meaning, and the design re-derives them for the new parent. Any other check that compares a path with `tempfile.gettempdir()` changes the same way. At `e94727ce` there is exactly one such check (`grep -n gettempdir` over the probe, matching lines).
    - **The change applies to future cycles only.** S1 (recorded at smoke commit `f7520e72`) and the 2026-10-20 S2 run were built in system temp. The S1 records stay on the branch as recorded, and the AC-1.9 re-smoke supersedes them. The S2 run is incident evidence only (FR-3).
    - **Residuals.** System temp stays readable under `strict`, and that includes other sessions' scratch directories. Only material that carries no calibration data may be there. A third-party tool's own temp writes are not controlled, including the temp writes of agy, codex, git and Python internals. Whether those carry prompt text is UNVERIFIED for agy and codex.
  - AC-11.3: **grok's prompt and temp files stay inside its root.**
    - The grok prompt is staged inside the dispatch's `--cd` directory, not with `mktemp -t`. It is removed when `exec` exits, on every path out of it. `strict` refuses a `--prompt-file` outside the working directory and system temp, so the prompt must be in the working directory.
    - Each grok child gets `TMPDIR=<root>/.tmp`, created by `exec` before the launch.
    - **Where the prompt and `TMPDIR` go, by kind of working directory** (orchestrator ruling; added in v1.13). The two bullets above describe a dispatch whose `--cd` is an export root, which is not a git work tree. That covers every calibration dispatch, and there the prompt and `<root>/.tmp` stay inside the root. In production, a non-calibration `exec grok` runs with its `--cd` directory inside a git work tree. Its staged prompt and its `TMPDIR` must then sit under `<git toplevel>/.h-mad/`, which the repository's `.gitignore` ignores (`.h-mad/*`; `git check-ignore -v` names that rule for a path under it at `37df3ccc`). They never go in a bare `<cd>/.tmp/` or anywhere else in the tree that is not ignored. The reason: an audit must leave `git status` unchanged, and an untracked `.tmp/` or prompt file is a tree delta. The design names the paths under `.h-mad/` and how they are removed when `exec` exits.
      - The axis is the working directory's kind, and it has exactly two values: inside a git work tree, or not. Inside a work tree means `git -C <cd> rev-parse --show-toplevel` succeeds. Residual: a work tree whose `.gitignore` does not ignore `.h-mad/` gets the same placement, and there the files are an untracked delta. The design states whether `exec` checks with `git check-ignore` and refuses.
      - Residual: when `--cd` is inside a work tree but is not its top level, `<git toplevel>/.h-mad/` lies outside grok's working directory. `strict` refuses a `--prompt-file` outside the working directory and system temp (grok exits 1; incident record table), so that launch fails closed. The design states whether `exec` refuses it up front instead.
    - **A grok pass must be able to deliver under the profile (AC-1.3).** At `e94727ce` the report path is `${stem}_p<i>.report.md` under the stem dir, which the calibration runbook places under `$HOME/.local/state/…` (design D8), and `strict` refuses writes there. Left unchanged, every strict grok pass loses `report-file` delivery. The design therefore places a grok pass's report path where the profile lets grok write, and the only such place outside system temp is inside the pass's root. Each pass of a cycle has its own root (AC-3.8), so a report written inside one pass's root is not inside any other pass's root (amended in v1.10; v1.9 left this cross-pass exposure open, with all passes in one root). What remains is AC-3.8 residual (1): codex and agy can still read a sibling root by absolute path, because their read bound is unverified.
    - Every file the wrapper places in the root (the staged prompt, `.tmp/`, any report path) is outside the content scan's precondition, because it is written after the AC-2.6 scan. It is never copied by `record-cycle` except through the cycle's own report and sidecar patterns. An S1 reset removes all of it (design D5).
  - AC-11.4: **Per-run preflight canary.** grok documents that when a sandbox cannot be applied it logs a warning and continues without enforcement, and nothing positively shows that a profile was applied (incident record). Before a run's first grok leg, so once per run directory, one grok call runs under the same profile the run's grok legs use: the same `--sandbox` value, the same `TMPDIR` rule, and a working directory created the way a root is under the run's `roots/`. That one call asks grok to write two files and to read one. The read was added in v1.11 by operator ruling 2026-10-20, within the same call and with no extra dispatch:
    - a **denied target**, at a fresh, never-existing path outside the canary's working directory and outside system temp, under the run directory;
    - a **positive control**, inside its working directory, at the same placement a grok report path takes (AC-11.3);
    - a **read sentinel**, which grok is asked to read and print. It is a file that the canary plants before the call, under the run directory, outside the canary's working directory, outside `roots/` and outside system temp. That mirrors where the originals (the live checkout, under `$HOME`) and the run state (under `$HOME/.local/state/`) live. Its content is a fresh random token, at least 128 bits, and it carries no calibration data. The prompt names the sentinel's path and never its content, so the token can reach the log only through a read.

    The canary ends on exactly one token line on stdout: `GROK-CANARY: PASS`, or `GROK-CANARY: REFUSED reason=<r>`. Callers read the token, never `$?`. **Exit contract** (orchestrator ruling on plan audit v4 p1, which found that v1.13 broke the audit-gate signal discipline; amended in v1.14): it exits 0 on `PASS` and on every `REFUSED` reason it determined, because a refusal is a measured verdict, not an operational error. It exits 2 only on a usage error, or on an internal error that escapes the canary. This spec defines no `internal` reason for the canary, so such an invocation may print no `GROK-CANARY:` line. An invocation that prints no `GROK-CANARY:` line is a refusal, whatever its exit code. No caller branches on the exit code. `<r>` is exactly one of these seven forms (five until v1.10), checked in this order, the first that applies winning:
    - `target_preexisting`: the denied target existed before the call, so the canary cannot judge it;
    - `escaped`: the denied target exists after the call, whatever grok's exit code or log says;
    - `read_escaped`: the sentinel's token appears anywhere in the canary log (any event, tool result or message), whatever grok's exit code says;
    - `cannot_run:<kind>`, where `<kind>` is exactly one of `grok_absent` (no `grok` on `PATH`), `grok_rc:<n>` (a non-zero exit, which includes an unknown profile name, measured rc 1; `<n>` is `hmad-dispatch exec`'s exit code: grok's own, except 3 for an exit 0 with no final message and 124 for the deadline (`timeout`); wording added in v1.13 from design v1.6), `timeout`, or `truncated` (the log has no `end` event);
    - `no_read_check:<kind>`: the content check cannot run, or cannot show anything. `<kind>` is exactly one of four:
      - `sentinel_unplanted`: the sentinel could not be freshly created with its token before the call. The canary then makes no grok call.
      - `log_unreadable`: the canary log is absent or cannot be read, so the token cannot be searched for.
      - `sentinel_changed`: after the call the sentinel is absent, or its content is not the planted token.
      - `not_attempted`: no `tool_call` in the log names the sentinel's path, so grok never tried the read and the token's absence proves nothing.
    - `no_control`: the positive control is absent, so grok never performed the write and the denial below proves nothing;
    - `no_denial`: the denied target is absent, but the log carries no `Operation not permitted` result for the attempted write.

    There is no other reason. Seven counts the forms above, with each parameterised form (`cannot_run`, `no_read_check`) as one. The read side has no denial-text condition. A read whose token stays out of the log, after a `tool_call` that named the sentinel, passes it. On any refusal no grok leg of that run dispatches, and the record is kept. The canary's prompt, log and token line, together with grok's binary path and `--version` line, are kept in the run directory, committed on the calibration branch with the run's first cycle record, and cited in the measurement doc (AC-6.5). They carry no calibration data. **Residuals, stated exactly:**
    - The canary proves two things for this run's grok binary and profile: one write to one path outside the working directory was denied, and one read of one path under the run directory did not reach the log. It does not prove that every path is denied. The read bound, which is what measurement validity rests on (AC-2.6 residual (3)), is also checked after the fact on every grok pass by the AC-6.6 scan.
    - The read check sees a read only through the log. grok logs tool results (`tool_call_update` `rawOutput`/`content`, Premises), but whether every result is logged in full is not measured. A read whose output grok never writes to the log, such as one it consumes without echoing, is not seen. The sentinel is not under system temp or the live checkout, so the canary does not test reads there: `strict` allows system-temp reads, and the live checkout's read refusal is quoted from the incident record, not re-tested per run.
    - A grok binary, profile or OS policy that changes after the canary within one run is not detected. The canary records the binary path and version, so a later reader can compare.
    - The denial text `Operation not permitted` is the macOS Seatbelt wording grok 1.0.62 relays. A future grok that words it differently is refused (`no_denial`), which fails closed.
    - The canary does not cover agy or codex.
  - AC-11.5: **What is not contained, stated as such.**
    - codex (`workspace-write`, not probed against a sibling root or the live checkout) and agy (no sandbox by default) have no verified read bound and no verified write bound outside their root. That is an UNVERIFIED residual (AC-2.6 residual (3)), and this feature does not claim them contained. What each of their standing passes named outside its root is published by the AC-6.6 scan.
    - `strict` leaves system temp readable, and grok's in-process network is not restricted, which this threat does not need.
    - When a grok working directory is the live checkout (an `exec grok` with no `--cd` falls back to the git top level), `strict` allows writes there. Calibration dispatches always pass a root.
    - An outer `/usr/bin/sandbox-exec` wrapper is out of scope (Out-of-Scope).
  - AC-11.6: **Tests.** Each of these gets its own test, each run with that case alone:
    - for AC-11.1, each of: no `--sandbox`, explicit `strict`, a weaker value refused without the override, a weaker value with the override recorded, `GROK_SANDBOX` set in the caller's environment and absent in the child's, the profile record per pass, and the `exec-pane` pin;
    - the AC-11.2 placement of each probe member, and the re-anchored reset guard (a target under system temp now refuses), with the guard's mutation killed;
    - the AC-11.3 prompt staging, its removal on exit, and `TMPDIR`;
    - one fixture per AC-11.4 reason form and per `kind` of each parameterised form, plus `PASS`, each built with a stub `grok` that plants that one outcome. `escaped` is planted with a log that does carry the denial text, so that the order is pinned. `read_escaped` gets two fixtures: one with the token in a tool result and one with it only in grok's message text. Both are planted with the positive control present and the denial text logged, so `read_escaped` is pinned ahead of the write-side reasons. A `PASS` fixture's prompt is asserted not to contain the token. Every fixture asserts the exit code as well as the token (v1.14): 0 for `PASS` and for every reason form, and 2 for a usage error and for an injected escaping exception, which are two more fixtures. A last pair asserts that a run printing no `GROK-CANARY:` line is read as a refusal under exit 0 and under exit 2.
    - for the AC-11.3 placement, one test per kind of working directory: an export root (prompt and `TMPDIR` in the root) and a git work tree (both under `<git toplevel>/.h-mad/`, with `git status --porcelain` unchanged after `exec` exits);
    - for AC-11.7, one fixture per refusal reason form (all six, `internal` among them, planted by an injected exception and, separately, by a usage error), plus a verified quarantine, each run against a stand-in `$HOME`. A fixture with a matching directory planted after the move asserts `residue:1`. Every fixture asserts the exit code as well as the token (v1.14): 0 for a verified quarantine and for each of the five ordered reasons, and 2 for both `internal` plantings, each still printing its `QUARANTINE: REFUSED reason=internal:<ExceptionName>` line.

    The live canary is not a test. It is the run's first grok call, made after the AC-11.7 quarantine.
  - AC-11.7: **grok session-store quarantine** (operator ruling 2026-10-20; added in v1.12). This is a requirement, and the run does not go ahead without it.
    - **When.** Twice in a run: before the AC-1.9 re-smoke, and again before the fresh S2's first cycle. Both times it comes before that stage's first grok call, and the first time it comes before the canary.
    - **What it moves.** Every directory matching `$HOME/.grok/sessions/*gas-root-*` that exists when the step starts is moved out of `$HOME/.grok/`. It is never deleted. It goes to a fresh quarantine directory under the run directory's parent, `$HOME/.local/state/h-mad/grok-audit-surface/`, outside every run directory's `roots/` and outside system temp. The design names the path form.
    - **Which directories that covers.** Before the re-smoke, the matching directories are those left by earlier runs. Before the fresh S2, they are any earlier run's that remain, plus the re-smoke's own, because the step moves every match present. Under `strict`, the quarantine sits where grok's reads are refused (the incident record table refuses reads under `$HOME/.local/state/`).
    - **Verification, fail closed.** The step lists the matching directories before the move and checks three things afterwards: no directory matching `$HOME/.grok/sessions/*gas-root-*` remains; each listed directory now exists in the quarantine; and the number moved equals the number listed. It ends on one token line that callers read, never `$?`. The design names the verb and the token. The outcome is either verified, with the count moved (0 is a valid count when nothing matched), or refused with exactly one of six reason forms. The first five are checked in this order:
      - `quarantine_exists`: the fresh quarantine path already exists;
      - `move_failed:<n>`: a move did not complete, where `<n>` is the number that failed;
      - `residue:<n>`: matching directories remain, where `<n>` is how many;
      - `count_mismatch`: the moved count and the listed count differ;
      - `unreadable`: the session store or the quarantine cannot be listed.

      A usage error or an exception that escapes the step prints `QUARANTINE: REFUSED reason=internal:<ExceptionName>`, which is a refusal like the five above. It is the sixth form, a last resort that none of the five names (added in v1.13 from design v1.6). There is no other reason. Six counts the forms, with each parameterised form (`move_failed`, `residue`, `internal`) as one. A refusal means the stage it guards does not start. No grok call of that run is made until a later run of the step verifies. **Exit contract** (amended in v1.14, same ruling as AC-11.4): the step exits 0 when it verifies and on every one of the five ordered reasons it determined. It exits 2 only on a usage error or an escaping internal error, and it still prints the `internal:<ExceptionName>` refusal line. An invocation that prints no `QUARANTINE:` line is a refusal. Callers read the token, never `$?`.
    - **Residuals, stated exactly.**
      - (1) Sessions that a cycle of the **same** run creates after the step are not moved. The narrowed AC-6.6 exemption is what covers those. A grok pass that names another session's directory is out of root and contaminated (`checkout`, v1.23), so it halts the run.
      - (2) grok sessions not named `gas-root-*` stay in the store, such as interactive or host-mode sessions keyed by the live checkout. A pass that names one is out of root under AC-6.6 and halts.
      - (3) Whether grok keeps another index or copy of session prompts outside `$HOME/.grok/sessions/` is not measured.
      - (4) Moving the directories may change how grok lists past sessions to an operator. The quarantine is kept, and moving a directory back is a manual act outside every run.

## Delivery by outcome
The outcome is read from token lines, never from `$?`: first the AC-1.7 `SMOKE:` line, then, only if it reads `SMOKE: PASS`, the AC-5.1 `SEEDSCORE:` line. Both components exit 0 on every verdict and 2 on UNREADABLE (AC-1.7, AC-5.1). A containment halt (AC-6.6), read from the scan's own token, comes before both: a halted run is not scored, whatever its `SMOKE:` line read (v1.10). Two more refusals come before both on the same footing (v1.20). The first is the assembler pin, the `ASSEMBLER:` token of design D21, read by `require --verb assembler`. Only `ASSEMBLER: PINNED` lets the run go on; `UNPINNED` and `NO_CLAUSE` stop it (AC-3.6). The second is the path-scope fail-closed halt, `REQUIRE: STOP reason=path_scope_missing:p<i>` (AC-3.9). A run stopped by either is not scored, whatever its `SMOKE:` line read. Every outcome ships the unconditional S0 set to `main`: the FR-5 probe and its synthetic AC-5.5 fixtures, the AC-8.1/8.2, AC-9.1–9.4 and FR-10 tests, AC-9.5, and AC-6.2a. FR-11 (v1.9) also ships on every outcome. So does AC-3.9 (v1.17): the assembler switch and clause, the wrapper's clause check, the assembler pin verbs and their tests. It ships before the AC-1.9 re-smoke, which cannot run without it (v1.20). FR-11 changes the wrapper and the probe, not the reading, and it ships before any further grok dispatch against a seeded document. AC-7.1–7.3 and AC-8.3 never ship before FR-11, even on `SEEDSCORE: PASS`. The FR-2 ledger and seeded copies exist on every outcome as well, because the FR-1 smoke's export root is built from them, but they go only to the calibration branch and never to `main` (AC-2.7).

| Outcome | Measurement doc | `grok-runtime.md` | SKILL.md unmeasured sites (AC-6.2) | Routing (AC-7.1–7.3) and AC-8.3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `SMOKE: UNREADABLE` | not published until the smoke derives again to `PASS` or `FAIL` (AC-1.7) | not shipped | not shipped | not shipped |
| `SMOKE: FAIL` | smoke result only, no `SEEDSCORE:` (AC-1.8) | smoke-failure sentence (AC-1.8) | audit precision stays unmeasured; pointer plus failure clause | not shipped |
| `SEEDSCORE: PASS` | full (FR-6) | AC-1.5 sentence | retired to the measured fractions | shipped |
| `SEEDSCORE: MISS` | full (FR-6) | AC-1.5 sentence | retired to the measured fractions | not shipped (AC-7.4) |
| `SEEDSCORE: INCONCLUSIVE` | full (FR-6), with `reason=` | AC-1.5 sentence | retired, saying the bar was not decidable and why (AC-6.3) | not shipped (AC-7.4) |

There is no other outcome. A smoke failure is not retried as a pass (AC-1.1). Any new smoke is a new reading at a new sha.

## Non-Functional Requirements
- Performance: the calibration costs 9 grok + 9 agy dispatches, plus 9 codex when available, plus at most one re-dispatch per document (AC-3.4), plus one canary grok call per run (AC-11.4). The FR-1 smoke costs one grok and one agy dispatch, plus one codex dispatch when codex is available (AC-1.6). The AC-1.9 re-smoke costs the same again, and the fresh S2 costs the full calibration again, because no dispatch of the 2026-10-20 S2 run is scored (FR-3). Each S2 pass builds its own export root (AC-3.8), which is one root build per pass (three per cycle with codex, two without) where there was one per cycle. The FR-1 timeout bounds each pass. In production, one more dispatch per document first gate after a PASS (FR-7). Revisions cost the same as today.
- Security: audits run in the AC-2.6 non-git export roots, which sit outside every git work tree and, from v1.9, outside system temp (AC-11.2). The roots are not read-only, and they do not bound what an agent reads or where it writes (AC-2.6 residual (3)). grok's reads and writes are bounded by its `strict` OS sandbox; the per-run canary tests one denied write and one denied read, and the AC-6.6 scan checks the read bound on every pass (FR-11, AC-6.6). codex and agy have no verified read or write bound, which is UNVERIFIED (AC-11.5). The export roots are never committed anywhere. All calibration data (the seeded copies, the ledger with its `original`, `seeded` and `expected` fields, the findings, the key, the labels, and every S1 and S2 cycle's reports, pass logs, kept prompts, captured agent output and captured wrapper stdout; AC-2.7) is committed only on `refs/heads/calibration/grok-audit-surface` and never merged into `main` (AC-2.7). The AC-2.7 main leak check enforces this, with its residuals stated there. The ledger and the labels hold no secrets.
- Compatibility: no change to `exit_check`, `round_check`, `combine`, `scan_grok` or the wrapper's `--surfaces` grammar. One wrapper behaviour does change for every caller, production included (FR-11, AC-11.1, AC-11.3): `exec grok` runs `strict` by default, refuses a weaker `--sandbox` without the override flag, and stages grok's prompt and sets the grok child's `TMPDIR`. In an export root both go in the root; inside a git work tree both go under the git-ignored `<git toplevel>/.h-mad/`, so `git status` is unchanged (AC-11.3, v1.13). A grok audit leg outside calibration whose report path lies outside its `--cd` and outside system temp would lose `report-file` delivery under `strict`. The design states which production paths, if any, that reaches. Production audit prompts are unchanged. With the AC-3.9 switch off they assemble byte-identically to the pre-v1.17 assembler (AC-3.9 test (4)), and only calibration cycles turn the switch on and add the path-scope clause (v1.18, replacing v1.17's every-caller change). The codex-unavailable routing and the `fallback_agent` semantics are unchanged.

## Out-of-Scope
- grok as Phase-5 implementer, as doc author, or as the subject of a TDD write gate (D1).
- The host-mode adapter: hooks, `spawn_subagent`, the TDD gate, session id and the 1.0.41 mapping line (D4).
- Replacing the doc-auditor delta review or agy with grok (brainstorm, Alternatives).
- Replaying the gateway-consolidation corpus.
- Changing the codex-unavailable route, or adding a `grok_status` state field.
- Seeding measurement-class, prose or ordering defects (AC-2.1 residual).
- Preventing agy or codex from reading or writing outside the export root by absolute path (AC-2.6 residual 3, AC-11.5; for grok this is FR-11, in scope from v1.9); an outer `/usr/bin/sandbox-exec` wrapper around any agent; and changing the codex-unavailable route after a failed smoke (AC-1.8 follow-up).
- Scoring, salvaging or re-labelling any cycle of the 2026-10-20 S2 run, which is incident evidence only (FR-3).
- Server-side protection of `refs/heads/calibration/grok-audit-surface` on origin. The spec states the failure mode instead (AC-2.7 `calibration_ref:<kind>`).

## Assumptions
- grok (whatever its version, D4) and agy are on PATH at calibration time. codex is optional, and when it is absent the reference ceiling is published as not run.
- The audit template's `class:` and `quote:` contract is the one at the calibration sha, and every surface receives it identically (AC-3.2). The prompt as a whole is not the one at the calibration sha: it is assembled from the h-mad checkout `$here` resolves to (AC-3.9). It is also not the production prompt: it is the production prompt plus exactly the AC-3.9 clause, which calibration cycles alone carry (v1.18). The measurement doc states that delta, and AC-3.6 records the assembler commit.
- The hand labeler can open the calibration tree and the assembled prompts, which FR-3 keeps.
- origin (`BrightGold70/skills`) accepts the push of `refs/heads/calibration/grok-audit-surface` and keeps it. If it loses or rewrites the ref, re-runs refuse with `calibration_ref:<kind>` (AC-2.7). They never fall back to a copy on `main`.
